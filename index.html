ARCHITECTURE RESEARCH · ACTIVE

Self-Organising Transformer

Can a Transformer learn its own structure while it learns its weights — which layers, heads, FFN groups and skip edges to keep — and end up cheaper than a dense model of the same quality?

Honest status: not yet. The gated models still trail a dense baseline, and I retracted an early "beats dense" result after tracing it to a library bug that broke checkpoint loading. The useful finding: a gate's size doesn't tell you how important a unit is (layer gates correlate at ρ = −0.72 with real importance; a Taylor estimate gets 0.93). Next up: a Phase 2 run (discrete birth and death of units) with a fix that stops dormant units quietly propping up the numbers.

EVALUATION · SAFETY · BUILDING

meta-eval

A harness that measures how fine-tuning and quantisation change model safety, and evaluates the evaluators: are common LLM-as-a-judge models biased on safety-critical tasks? A self-audit already caught judges sharing weights with the models they scored. No results yet — the full judge run is next.

Model Understanding

Tools for poking at what transformers attend to and why: saliency maps, integrated gradients, logit lens and attention rollout across classification, generative and seq2seq models.

COMPUTER VISION · EXTENSION

DiffVax extension

Extending the authors' ICLR 2026 image-immunisation method to SD3.5 and FLUX. Verification tests caught silent training bugs — severed gradient paths and an inverted loss term — that produced plausible loss curves and zero signal. Verified on CPU; GPU validation pending.

About

Software engineer turned AI engineer, with a habit of going one layer deeper than strictly necessary.

I studied Software Engineering at the University of Portsmouth and started out on automation and computer vision. From there I moved through ML engineering into LLMs, agents and production AI — and spent a stint managing an AI team before deciding I wanted my hands back on the hard problems.

What I care about: evidence over assertion, simple systems, honest numbers, and using code when the model has no real decision to make. Increasingly, that means evaluation — most people can build an agent, fewer can tell you how much to trust the thing that grades it.
